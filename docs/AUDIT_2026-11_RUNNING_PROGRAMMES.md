# Running programme audit — November 2026

**Audited source:** [merged Piece 5B, 7823372c](https://github.com/mmwiza1/Race-day-dreams/commit/7823372c81d3fe09c9485ed6de5ee461d546f593), Android 0.16.0-test1 / 29 and iOS 0.16.0 / 29. Independent audit performed after the merge. All example runs and workouts in this report are synthetic.

## Executive summary

Race Day Dreams is ready for development testing as a local running coach. Its plans are useful starting points when the entered training history is current and the runner can keep the scheduled days. The revised generator can build convincing 5K, 10K, half-marathon and marathon programmes with easy mileage, recovery weeks, race-specific work and a taper. New or returning runners get materially different prescriptions. The app exposes the evidence for weekly changes, preserves training history and leaves decisions with the runner.

Two behavioural discrepancies remain. A marathon readiness card can give contradictory advice when recent evidence is absent. Two otherwise equivalent two-day schedules can produce very different amounts of quality work, depending on how the runner chose the frequency. The app also needs clearer limits on how much its readiness rating and projected future mileage tell a runner about what they will actually tolerate.

The half-marathon fixture cut week three to four from 22.4 km to 18.0 km. Its final four training weeks were 34.0, 34.0, 25.5 and 11.4 km excluding the event. The experienced marathon case cut 67.9 km to 54.4 km in week four, then finished with 80.8, 68.4, 60.4 and 32.4 km before adding the race. These outputs support the presence of a cutback and taper, without establishing that the individual progression is tolerable in practice.

### The reported 14.7 km example

The earlier 25-week engine output was reproduced with a **plausible baseline fixture**, 19.6 km/week, 9 km longest recent run, eight consistent weeks, three specified running days and a 90-minute budget. This reproduces the old high week of 30.7 km and 14.7 km longest run. The old cap allowed no more than about 48% of that week, and 19.6 km/week was below the old 20 km quality gate. This shows how the reported output can occur. That user's exact stored baseline, effort history, unavailable dates and goal settings were unavailable.

The merged build gives **Monday 26 April 17.0 km long, Thursday 29 April 8.5 km easy, Friday 30 April 8.5 km including half-marathon effort**, with the event on 16 May 2027. The plan reaches 17 km before this week as well. With three runs, the guided long-run share is 50% of this 34 km week. The long run also obeys the distance-specific ceiling and its progression limit. The quality work stays effort-based because this fixture has no performance result. The target of about 5:41/km is a race goal and does not become a workout pace by itself. This is an observed fixture result, not a prescription or forecast for the original runner. [Comparison and limits](TRAINING_PROGRAMME_PLAN.md#candidate-comparison-against-the-previous-generator)

## Journey and coaching behaviour

| Stage | What the implementation supports | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onboarding and baseline | Manual recent weekly volume, frequency, longest run and evidence can work without Health Connect or Apple Health. Missing/stale evidence sends new plans towards run/walk. A recent update of manual evidence is a different signal from a continuous run history. | Conservative and usable without a wearable. Users who import only recent workouts may need help understanding why signs of a longer history are missing. |
| Plan generation and scheduling | Availability is a hard input. The preferred long day is a soft constraint. Actual hard lower-body work costs more than mere gym availability. Quality takes recovery spacing and the selected approach into account. | The fixture remained usable on fixed days. Two nearby easy days can legitimately occur if availability is tight. Aim to explain which preference moved a long run. |
| Phase, volume and taper | Established profiles use about 7% growth per building week, bounded to 0.5–3 km, with a cap derived from event, frequency and current volume. Every fourth non-taper week normally cuts to 80%. The tapered weeks use distance-specific factors. Planned long-run growth is bounded by recent long exposure, distance and weekly share. | Sensible guardrails for a projection. Growth after a cutback compares against the unreduced level. A projected long run is not itself evidence of increased capacity. The tests protect the accepted-rebuild ceiling. |
| Quality and paces | Run/walk, Distance First, Balanced, Performance and Zone 2 materially affect the programme. Familiar shorter work can appear early in a taper. Effort is retained without a suitable performance result. Time-limited runs can lose repetitions or stay easy. | Gives established runners useful variety. A setting called Performance does not override evidence thresholds. Numeric pace remains model-derived and should be read with the available evidence. |
| Workout and race day | Shared execution advances the target steps. Android retained measured interval/paused recovery evidence; iOS advanced without pretending GPS movement had occurred. Run Score, splits and run details are separate from prescriptions. | Start and timer paths verified with simulated devices. Outdoor cue timing, audio, GPS, finishing accuracy and long-duration fatigue were unverified. |
| Reviews, missed days and imports | The review uses linked workouts and actual recorded volume, respects deliberate skips/moves, identifies recurring missed days, and offers keep/rebuild/return or modest progress only on qualifying evidence. Native and matching watch evidence can count once. | A review never silently applies a plan. Imports, incomplete data and genuine breaks can look similar. Keep is a valid decision; the review should emphasise uncertainty rather than imply all is going to plan. |
| Race performance | A lightweight projection uses the closest evidence and a Riegel-style relationship for a target-time check. Readiness also considers the plan's scheduled volume and horizon. | Useful to flag ambition, limited as a predictor. It does not establish injury risk, day-of readiness, course suitability or the chance of achieving a time. |

The separate Race Day prediction is stricter than the readiness card. It needs at least two qualifying performance results, and for the half and marathon also needs recent endurance coverage. Its estimate range and confidence use product heuristics and do not change the entered goal. The live strategy can show a closing pace to achieve the user's time after a conservative start. Required arithmetic pace and predicted ability should remain visibly separate. Run Score explains an individual run using observed evidence; the weekly review does not treat that score as a fitness measurement. ## Findings and proposed remediation

| ID and severity | Reproduction and evidence | Recommended action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R-01 · P1 · Contradictory readiness advice** | Set a marathon target (e.g. 3:30), three allowed runs, no recent history and a race five weeks away. The plan was rated **Not supported**, with a 19-minute longest run/walk, yet a reason read “Your plan prepares you to finish”. The statement is appended unconditionally when a target has no recent race time. A runner could reasonably act on the more reassuring advice. [PlanFeasibility](../sharedDomain/src/commonMain/kotlin/org/racedaydreams/domain/coaching/PlanFeasibility.kt) | **High impact, small patch.** Derive the evidence sentence from the preparation result or omit the finish claim. Test each rating against both absent and ambiguous time evidence, including 5K run/walk and marathon, and inspect the card on both platforms. |
| **R-02 · P2 · Frequency-specific intensity changes with the way it is selected** | Generate a 25-week Performance half on Monday/Thursday/Friday from 30 km/week, 12 km longest run and eight consistent weeks. Request three days and select Fewer longer runs. The plan has two actual days and 19 quality sessions. Request two days with Balanced distribution and identical history. It has two days, the same peak and long run, and no quality sessions. The generator computes quality readiness from requested days before reducing scheduling frequency. [Generator](../sharedDomain/src/commonMain/kotlin/org/racedaydreams/domain/coaching/OriginalTrainingPlanGenerator.kt) | **High impact, medium patch.** Apply explicit intensity rules to effective frequency. If two-day Performance work is intentional, support it transparently in both modes and test week-level spacing, optional cross-training and restricted weeks. |
| **R-03 · P2 · Remaining-time wording uses the original horizon** | Inspect a short generated race plan well after its start. The reason is built from race date minus `plan.startDate()` and says “weeks until race day”. The assessment has no as-of argument, so the wording remains the original number. The forecast is useful, but the label implies a live countdown. [PlanFeasibility](../sharedDomain/src/commonMain/kotlin/org/racedaydreams/domain/coaching/PlanFeasibility.kt) | **Medium impact, small patch.** Say “your plan allows … weeks” for the original horizon or supply an explicit as-of date and define what should be reassessed. Include late plan views and partial calendar weeks. |
| **R-04 · P2 · iOS lacks an interval-by-interval review after a run** | The simulator executes the timed step. In source, Android detail includes planned-step review and long-run durability, while the iOS detail includes overall feedback and comparison without those sections. A runner cannot verify the same recorded workout evidence from the iOS UI. [Platform parity](PLATFORM_PARITY.md) | **Medium impact, medium patch.** Use the same display of completed, skipped and unfinished steps, with provenance for old runs without samples. Test structured runs on both platforms. |
| **R-05 · P2 · Long-term workload after a rebuild is governed by a ceiling** | Accept a lower rebuild after four weeks of shorter running. The old week-five jump is fixed. Subsequent weeks can remain below the old progression until new accepted evidence. The UI needs to help a runner see that remaining work also changed and when to open another review. This is a design limitation rather than a reproduced incorrect target. [BlockReassessment](../sharedDomain/src/commonMain/kotlin/org/racedaydreams/domain/coaching/BlockReassessment.kt) | **Medium impact, medium patch.** Make the longer-term ceiling and next evidence checkpoint visible. Keep approvals explicit, preserve completed plans and run a second interrupted rebuild on top of an already accepted one. |
| **R-06 · P3 · Benchmarks are approximate** | Readiness applies heuristic volume/long-run references and estimated results from shorter distances. No matched completion outcomes establish a universal adequate distance, or show that a 10% recent-long-run guide is an injury guarantee. | **Medium impact, small initial documentation.** Show which input determined the label, how recent it is, and which alternative changes the biggest constraint. Keep no-baseline runners away from a precise forecast. |

| **R-07 · P2 · Cross-provider overlap can group a separate nested run** | The domain probe passed a native 75-minute 12 km run and a separately identified Apple Health 20-minute 3 km recording, wholly inside it. Grouping returned one counted session and retained both originals. This is correct for a duplicate view of one run. A separate warm-up or assessment recorded by a second writer can look identical to this heuristic. [Grouping rule](../sharedDomain/src/commonMain/kotlin/org/racedaydreams/domain/activity/PhysicalRunSessions.kt) | **Medium impact, medium effort.** Expose grouped source records and consider a user override or a distance/type check for materially nested observations. Preserve existing canonical identity and do not double count verified duplicates. |

No P0 was reproduced. R-01 should be addressed before asking unprepared runners to rely on marathon readiness. This audit proposes review and a separate fix PR. It makes no code changes.

## What remains unverified

- Physically running all five programme types through to race day, including varied terrain, turns, tunnels, carrying a phone, poor GPS, pace alerts and head-unit or watch behaviour.
- Real workout arrival and revocation from Health Connect and HealthKit, duplicate conditions with multiple writers and routes, and day-boundary inputs across travel/daylight-saving changes.
- Effectiveness and injury outcomes for beginners after twelve months, expected maximal training tolerances, performance-specific workloads for advanced runners and subjective quality of written cues.
- An extended TalkBack/VoiceOver walkthrough for onboarding, readiness ideas, timed controls, plan links and stop/recovery actions. Automated semantics and 200% captures cover selected cases only.
