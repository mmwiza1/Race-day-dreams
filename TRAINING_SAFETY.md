# Training safety

Race Day Dreams provides fitness guidance, not medical care. It does not diagnose
conditions, guarantee race outcomes or promise to prevent injury.

The current app collects goals, routine and optional running evidence. It does
not yet generate training prescriptions, evaluate recovery or predict race results. A calculated target pace
is arithmetic from the runner's selected finish time, not an assessment that the
pace is suitable.

## Language and runner control

Use recommendations and ordinary explanations, never diagnoses or shame. Missing
a session is normal. Prefer “Workout not completed” and “Your week changed” to
failure labels. A runner may keep a goal or decline a training adjustment.

For future coaching, every significant adjustment must show:

1. Recommendation.
2. Why it was proposed.
3. Alternative options.
4. The runner's decision.
5. Recalculation using what the runner actually did.

Rest recommendations must not disable the ability to record or complete a run.
Do not punish an override by labelling it failure or removing later choices.

## Concerning symptoms

If a runner reports chest pain, fainting, severe difficulty breathing or serious
injury symptoms, advise them to stop exercise and seek appropriate medical
advice. Do not suggest pushing through and do not diagnose the cause.

The app is not an emergency service and does not monitor symptoms.

## Claims to avoid

- “You are injured/sick” based on training or recovery data.
- “This programme prevents injury” or guarantees health safety.
- A readiness percentage described as a scientific probability of success.
- An exact predicted result inferred from a single fast workout.
- Generic HR formulas presented as the runner's verified personal zones.

Describe Zone 2 / Aerobic as a lower-intensity training option designed to build
aerobic endurance while limiting unnecessary high-intensity workload. Missing
heart-rate data should allow conversational-effort/RPE guidance when that
training feature is implemented.

## Evidence quality and conservative thresholds

Baseline confidence describes amount, recency and completeness of records; it is
not a probability of success. Performance-strategy eligibility is recomputed from
recent evidence (including explicitly reviewed manual history), rather than a
permanent flag. Its workload/consistency thresholds are transparent product
heuristics, not medical screening or universal training requirements.

An assessment is optional. Controlled effort and a shorter distance remain
available, particularly for new or returning runners. A single 1K or 5K result
does not establish long-distance endurance or dictate a training plan.

Rolling-effort and split quality checks reject jumps, long gaps and pause
discontinuities. Those checks concern data reliability; passing them is not a
health or injury assessment. Manual/official results reflect what the runner
entered and are labelled accordingly.

Shoe-distance guidance suggests checking cushioning/outsole feel progressively.
It does not declare a shoe unsafe at a universal mileage. Preparation reminders
use general food, hydration, sleep and kit advice without fixed fluid quantities,
supplements or universal carb-loading rules.
